1 Equipment & Shopping List

Everything you need to buy — use a pre-built RC car to keep it simple.

🚗 The Car

1

Pre-Built RC Car

Any cheap 1:18 or 1:16 scale

Any remote-controlled car from a local toy shop or Amazon. You'll drive it with its own remote — the ESP32 just rides on top as a passenger, reading sensors.

Look for: Flat roof/surface for mounting, decent size (not too small), works on 3–6V batteries.

₹300 – ₹800

🧠 Core Electronics

2

ESP32 DevKit V1

ESP-WROOM-32 · 30-pin

Dual-core 240MHz MCU with built-in Wi-Fi & Bluetooth. The brain of the IoT system. Connects to your laptop's Wi-Fi and sends sensor data via WebSocket.

₹400 – ₹500
3

MPU6050 (GY-521)

3-axis Accel + 3-axis Gyro · I2C

6-axis motion sensor. Reads acceleration (G-force) on X/Y/Z axes and angular velocity. This is the crash detector — a sudden spike in G-force means impact.

₹150 – ₹200
4

NEO-6M GPS Module

u-blox NEO-6M · UART · 3.3V

Satellite GPS receiver with ceramic antenna. Outputs NMEA sentences with lat/lng, speed, altitude. Needs clear sky — works outdoors. Indoor fallback uses default coords.

₹350 – ₹500

⚡ Power & Connectivity

5

USB Power Bank (Small)

5V · 1A+ · ≤100g

Powers the ESP32 separately from the car's motors. A small lipstick-style power bank works great. Velcro it to the car's roof. Must have micro-USB or USB-C output.

₹200 – ₹400
6

Jumper Wires

M-F × 20, M-M × 20 · 20cm

For connecting ESP32 to MPU6050 and GPS. Male-Female for module pins, Male-Male for breadboard. 20cm length is ideal for mounting flexibility.

₹80
7

Mini Breadboard

400-point · 55×85mm

Optional but recommended. Lets you connect all components without soldering. Mount on car with double-sided tape. Can be skipped if you solder directly.

₹50
8

Micro-USB Cable

Data cable (not charge-only!)

For flashing firmware from laptop to ESP32. MUST be a data cable — charge-only cables won't work. You probably already have one.

₹50

🔊 Optional Add-ons

9

Red LED + Buzzer

5mm LED + 5V active buzzer

Visual and audio crash indicator on the car itself. LED flashes red and buzzer beeps when a crash is detected. Great for demo visibility.

₹30
10

Mounting Supplies

Velcro strips + double-sided tape

For attaching ESP32, breadboard, power bank, and GPS module to the RC car. Velcro is best — lets you remove/reattach components easily.

₹30

2 Component Pinout Reference

Know your pins before wiring anything.

ESP32 DevKit V1 — Pin Map (30-pin)

⚠️
Only certain GPIO pins are safe to use. Pins 6–11 are connected to internal flash — never use them. Pins 34, 35, 36, 39 are input-only (no output).
We Use GPIO Pin Function Connected To
✅ GPIO 21 I2C SDA (default) MPU6050 SDA
✅ GPIO 22 I2C SCL (default) MPU6050 SCL
✅ GPIO 16 UART2 RX GPS TX
✅ GPIO 17 UART2 TX GPS RX
✅ GPIO 2 Onboard LED Status LED
✅ GPIO 4 Digital Out Buzzer (+)
✅ 3.3V 3.3V Power Out MPU6050 VCC, GPS VCC
✅ GND Ground All GND pins

NEO-6M GPS — 4 Pins

PinFunctionConnect ToNotes
VCCPowerESP32 3.3V3.3V or 5V (check your module)
GNDGroundESP32 GNDCommon ground

🔴
CRITICAL — TX ↔ RX Crossover: GPS TX connects to ESP32 RX (GPIO 16). GPS RX connects to ESP32 TX (GPIO 17). They CROSS — this is NOT a mistake!

3 Complete Wiring Connections

Every single wire you need to connect, with color coding.

Wire Color Legend

Red = 3.3V Power
Black = Ground (GND)
Green = I2C SDA (Data)
Blue = I2C SCL (Clock)
Orange = UART TX/RX
Yellow = Signal / LED / Buzzer

Connection A — MPU6050 (Accelerometer + Gyroscope)

Protocol: I2C — Only 4 wires needed.

Wire # Color From (ESP32) To (MPU6050) Signal
A1 Red 3.3V VCC Power
A2 Black GND GND Ground
A3 Green GPIO 21 SDA I2C Data
A4 Blue GPIO 22 SCL I2C Clock

Connection B — NEO-6M GPS Module

Protocol: UART (Serial) — Only 4 wires needed.

Wire # Color From (ESP32) To (NEO-6M) Signal
B1 Red 3.3V VCC Power
B2 Black GND GND Ground
B3 Orange GPIO 16 (RX2) TX GPS data → ESP32
B4 Lt. Orange GPIO 17 (TX2) RX ESP32 cmds → GPS

Connection C — LED + Buzzer (Optional)

Wire # Color From (ESP32) To Signal
C1 Yellow GPIO 2 LED Anode (+) (long leg) Crash indicator
C2 Black GND LED Cathode (–) (short leg) Ground (via 220Ω resistor)
C3 Yellow GPIO 4 Buzzer (+) Crash alarm
C4 Black GND Buzzer (–) Ground
✅
Total wires: Only 8 wires for the essential setup (MPU6050 + GPS). Add 4 more for LED + buzzer = 12 wires total. That's it!
⚠️
Shared Power Rail: Both MPU6050 and GPS share the same 3.3V and GND pins from ESP32. Use a breadboard to split the 3.3V and GND lines, or connect both VCC wires to the same 3.3V pin.

5 Physical Assembly on RC Car

Step-by-step guide to mounting everything on the car.

1

Prepare the RC Car

Open the RC car and ensure it works with its remote. Identify a flat area on the roof/top where you can mount the breadboard. Clean the surface.

2

Mount the Mini Breadboard

Peel the adhesive backing off the breadboard and stick it to the top of the RC car. If no adhesive, use velcro strips or double-sided tape. Place it centrally for balance.

3

Place ESP32 on Breadboard

Push the ESP32 DevKit pins into the breadboard, straddling the center divider. The USB port should face outward (toward the rear of the car) for easy access.

4

Connect MPU6050

Wire the MPU6050 using 4 jumper wires (VCC→3.3V, GND→GND, SDA→GPIO21, SCL→GPIO22). Mount the sensor flat on the car body — orientation matters! The chip-face should point UP, with the X-axis pointing forward (toward the front of the car).

5

Connect GPS Module

Wire the NEO-6M with 4 wires (VCC→3.3V, GND→GND, TX→GPIO16, RX→GPIO17). Mount the GPS module with its ceramic antenna facing skyward (the square patch antenna on top). Tape it to the highest point on the car.

6

Add LED + Buzzer (Optional)

Connect the LED (GPIO 2 → 220Ω resistor → LED anode → LED cathode → GND) and buzzer (GPIO 4 → buzzer+ → buzzer– → GND). Mount them where they're visible.

7

Attach Power Bank

Velcro or tape the USB power bank to the car. Connect it to ESP32 via micro-USB/USB-C cable. This powers the ESP32 and all sensors independently from the car's motors.

8

Secure All Wires

Use small cable ties or tape to secure loose jumper wires to the car body. Make sure nothing hangs over the wheels or interferes with the car's movement.

🔴
MPU6050 Orientation is Critical! The sensor must be mounted flat and rigid. Any tilt or loose mounting will give noisy readings and false crash detections. Use foam tape for vibration dampening if the car vibrates a lot.
💡
Weight Balance: Place the power bank near the center of the car. Heavy components at the edges can make the car tip during turns.

6 Arduino IDE Setup & Libraries

One-time setup to flash firmware to the ESP32.

Step 1 — Install Arduino IDE

1

Download Arduino IDE 2.x

Go to https://www.arduino.cc/en/software → Download Arduino IDE 2.x for Windows. Install with default settings.

Step 2 — Add ESP32 Board Support

2

Open Arduino IDE → File → Preferences

In the "Additional boards manager URLs" field, paste this URL:
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/gh-pages/package_esp32_index.json

3

Install ESP32 Board Package

Go to Tools → Board → Boards Manager. Search for "esp32". Install "esp32 by Espressif Systems" (version 2.x or 3.x).

4

Select Your Board

Tools → Board → esp32 → "ESP32 Dev Module". Leave all other settings at default (Upload Speed: 921600, Flash Size: 4MB, etc.).

Step 3 — Install Required Libraries

Go to Sketch → Include Library → Manage Libraries and install each of these:

Library Name Author Purpose
WebSockets Markus Sattler (Links2004) WebSocket client to connect to M2 server
ArduinoJson Benoit Blanchon Build and parse JSON payloads
TinyGPSPlus Mikal Hart Parse NMEA sentences from GPS
Adafruit MPU6050 Adafruit Read accelerometer/gyroscope data
Adafruit Unified Sensor Adafruit Dependency for MPU6050 library
PubSubClient Nick O'Leary MQTT publish/subscribe client

Step 4 — Install USB Driver (if needed)

🔌
Most ESP32 DevKit V1 boards use a CP2102 or CH340 USB-to-Serial chip. If your computer doesn't recognize the board:
  • CP2102: Download driver from silabs.com/developers/usb-to-uart-bridge-vcp-drivers
  • CH340: Download driver from sparks.fun/ch340
After installing, check Device Manager → Ports (COM) for a new COM port.

Step 5 — Test the Connection

5

Verify ESP32 is detected

Connect ESP32 via USB. In Arduino IDE: Tools → Port → Select the COM port that appeared (e.g., COM3, COM5). Upload a blank sketch to test — it should compile and upload with "Done uploading" message.

#define BUZZER_DURATION_MS      2000   // How long buzzer sounds on crash

// ── Telemetry Timing ───────────────────────────────────────

#define SENSOR_READ_INTERVAL_MS 20     // Read MPU6050 every 20ms (50Hz)

// ── Default GPS (fallback when no satellite fix) ───────────
#define DEFAULT_LAT   26.2183    // Gwalior, India
#define DEFAULT_LNG   78.1828

#endif

8 ESP32 Firmware — firmware.ino

The main Arduino sketch that runs on the ESP32 inside the toy car.

firmware.ino
// ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
//  Segura SOS — ESP32 IoT Car Firmware
//  Reads MPU6050 + GPS, sends data to M2 via WebSocket + MQTT
// ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

#include <WiFi.h>
#include <WebSocketsClient.h>
#include <ArduinoJson.h>
#include <Wire.h>
#include <Adafruit_MPU6050.h>
#include <Adafruit_Sensor.h>
#include <TinyGPSPlus.h>
#include <PubSubClient.h>
#include "config.h"

// ── Objects ────────────────────────────────────────────────
Adafruit_MPU6050 mpu;
TinyGPSPlus gps;
HardwareSerial gpsSerial(2);  // UART2

WiFiClient espClient;
WiFiClient mqttWifiClient;
PubSubClient mqttClient(mqttWifiClient);
WebSocketsClient webSocket;

// ── State Variables ────────────────────────────────────────
float currentG = 1.0;
float accelX = 0, accelY = 0, accelZ = 0;
float gyroX = 0, gyroY = 0, gyroZ = 0;
float gpsLat = DEFAULT_LAT;
float gpsLng = DEFAULT_LNG;
float gpsSpeed = 0.0;
bool  gpsFix = false;

unsigned long lastTelemetry = 0;
unsigned long lastSensorRead = 0;
unsigned long lastCrashTime = 0;
unsigned long buzzerOffTime = 0;
bool wsConnected = false;
int eventCounter = 0;

// ── Generate unique event ID ───────────────────────────────
String generateEventId() {
    eventCounter++;
    return "iot-" + String(millis()) + "-" + String(eventCounter);
}

// ── WebSocket event handler ────────────────────────────────
void webSocketEvent(WStype_t type, uint8_t* payload, size_t length) {
    switch (type) {
        case WStype_DISCONNECTED:
            Serial.println("[WS] Disconnected");
            wsConnected = false;
            break;
        case WStype_CONNECTED:
            Serial.println("[WS] Connected to M2!");
            wsConnected = true;
            break;
        case WStype_TEXT:
            Serial.printf("[WS] Received: %s\n", payload);
            // Parse analysis results from M2 (optional: display on OLED)
            break;
    }
}

// ── MQTT callback (for receiving commands) ─────────────────
void mqttCallback(char* topic, byte* payload, unsigned int length) {
    String message;
    for (unsigned int i = 0; i < length; i++) {
        message += (char)payload[i];
    }
    Serial.printf("[MQTT] %s: %s\n", topic, message.c_str());
}

// ── Connect to MQTT broker ─────────────────────────────────
void reconnectMQTT() {
    if (!mqttClient.connected()) {
        Serial.print("[MQTT] Connecting...");
        if (mqttClient.connect(MQTT_CLIENT_ID)) {
            Serial.println(" connected!");
            mqttClient.subscribe(MQTT_TOPIC_COMMANDS);
        } else {
            Serial.printf(" failed (rc=%d)\n", mqttClient.state());
        }
    }
}

// ── Read MPU6050 Sensor ────────────────────────────────────
void readMPU6050() {
    sensors_event_t a, g, temp;
    mpu.getEvent(&a, &g, &temp);

    // Raw acceleration in m/s² → convert to G
    accelX = a.acceleration.x / 9.81;
    accelY = a.acceleration.y / 9.81;
    accelZ = a.acceleration.z / 9.81;

    // Total G-force magnitude
    currentG = sqrt(accelX * accelX + accelY * accelY + accelZ * accelZ);

    // Gyroscope data (rad/s)
    gyroX = g.gyro.x;
    gyroY = g.gyro.y;
    gyroZ = g.gyro.z;
}

// ── Read GPS ───────────────────────────────────────────────
void readGPS() {
    while (gpsSerial.available() > 0) {
        gps.encode(gpsSerial.read());
    }

    if (gps.location.isValid()) {
        gpsLat = gps.location.lat();
        gpsLng = gps.location.lng();
        gpsFix = true;
    }

    if (gps.speed.isValid()) {
        gpsSpeed = gps.speed.kmph();
    }
}

// ── Determine event type based on G-force ──────────────────
String classifyEvent(float g) {
    if (g > CRASH_G_THRESHOLD) return "crash";
    if (g > HARD_BRAKE_G_THRESHOLD) return "hard_brake";
    return "normal";
}

// ── Trigger crash alarm (LED + Buzzer) ──────────────────────
void triggerCrashAlarm() {
    digitalWrite(PIN_LED, HIGH);
    digitalWrite(PIN_BUZZER, HIGH);
    buzzerOffTime = millis() + BUZZER_DURATION_MS;
    Serial.println("🚨 CRASH DETECTED! Alarm triggered.");
}

// ── Build and send telemetry JSON ───────────────────────────
void sendTelemetry(String eventType) {
    StaticJsonDocument<512> doc;

    doc["event_id"]  = generateEventId();
    doc["timestamp"] = String(millis());  // M2 will re-timestamp
    doc["type"]      = eventType;
    doc["source"]    = "iot";
    doc["speed_kmh"] = gpsSpeed;
    doc["impact_g"]  = currentG;
    doc["weather"]   = "clear";  // Can add weather sensor later

    JsonObject gpsObj = doc.createNestedObject("gps");
    gpsObj["lat"] = gpsLat;
    gpsObj["lng"] = gpsLng;
    gpsObj["accuracy_m"] = gpsFix ? 5.0 : 100.0;

    // Add raw accelerometer data for richer telemetry
    JsonObject rawAccel = doc.createNestedObject("raw_accel");
    rawAccel["x"] = accelX;
    rawAccel["y"] = accelY;
    rawAccel["z"] = accelZ;

    String json;
    serializeJson(doc, json);

    // Send via WebSocket (primary channel)
    if (wsConnected) {
        webSocket.sendTXT(json);
        Serial.printf("[WS] Sent: %s → %s (G=%.2f)\n",
                       eventType.c_str(), gpsFix ? "GPS" : "default", currentG);
    }

    // Send via MQTT (backup channel)
    if (mqttClient.connected()) {
        mqttClient.publish(MQTT_TOPIC_TELEMETRY, json.c_str());
    }
}

// ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
//  SETUP
// ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
void setup() {
    Serial.begin(115200);
    Serial.println("\n═══ Segura SOS — IoT Car Starting ═══");

    // ── Pin setup ─────────────────────────────────────────
    pinMode(PIN_LED, OUTPUT);
    pinMode(PIN_BUZZER, OUTPUT);
    digitalWrite(PIN_LED, LOW);
    digitalWrite(PIN_BUZZER, LOW);

    // ── I2C + MPU6050 ─────────────────────────────────────
    Wire.begin(PIN_SDA, PIN_SCL);
    if (!mpu.begin()) {
        Serial.println("[ERROR] MPU6050 not found! Check wiring.");
        while (1) {
            digitalWrite(PIN_LED, !digitalRead(PIN_LED));
            delay(200);  // Blink LED rapidly = error
        }
    }
    Serial.println("[OK] MPU6050 initialized");
    mpu.setAccelerometerRange(MPU6050_RANGE_8_G);
    mpu.setGyroRange(MPU6050_RANGE_500_DEG);
    mpu.setFilterBandwidth(MPU6050_BAND_21_HZ);

    // ── GPS (UART2) ───────────────────────────────────────
    gpsSerial.begin(GPS_BAUD, SERIAL_8N1, PIN_GPS_RX, PIN_GPS_TX);
    Serial.println("[OK] GPS UART initialized");

    // ── Wi-Fi ─────────────────────────────────────────────
    Serial.printf("[WiFi] Connecting to %s", WIFI_SSID);
    WiFi.begin(WIFI_SSID, WIFI_PASSWORD);
    int attempts = 0;
    while (WiFi.status() != WL_CONNECTED && attempts < 30) {
        delay(500);
        Serial.print(".");
        attempts++;
    }
    if (WiFi.status() == WL_CONNECTED) {
        Serial.printf("\n[WiFi] Connected! IP: %s\n", WiFi.localIP().toString().c_str());
    } else {
        Serial.println("\n[WiFi] FAILED! Running in offline mode.");
    }

    // ── WebSocket → M2 ────────────────────────────────────
    webSocket.begin(SERVER_IP, SERVER_WS_PORT, SERVER_WS_PATH);
    webSocket.onEvent(webSocketEvent);
    webSocket.setReconnectInterval(3000);
    Serial.printf("[WS] Target: ws://%s:%d%s\n", SERVER_IP, SERVER_WS_PORT, SERVER_WS_PATH);

    // ── MQTT ──────────────────────────────────────────────
    mqttClient.setServer(MQTT_BROKER, MQTT_PORT);
    mqttClient.setCallback(mqttCallback);
    Serial.printf("[MQTT] Broker: %s:%d\n", MQTT_BROKER, MQTT_PORT);

    // ── Ready! ────────────────────────────────────────────
    digitalWrite(PIN_LED, HIGH);
    delay(500);
    digitalWrite(PIN_LED, LOW);
    Serial.println("═══ IoT Car Ready! ═══\n");
}

// ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
//  MAIN LOOP
// ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
void loop() {
    webSocket.loop();
    mqttClient.loop();

    // Reconnect MQTT if needed
    if (!mqttClient.connected()) {
        reconnectMQTT();
    }

    unsigned long now = millis();

    // ── Read sensors at 50Hz ──────────────────────────────
    if (now - lastSensorRead >= SENSOR_READ_INTERVAL_MS) {
        lastSensorRead = now;
        readMPU6050();
        readGPS();

        // ── Local crash detection ─────────────────────────
        if (currentG > CRASH_G_THRESHOLD &&
            (now - lastCrashTime) > CRASH_COOLDOWN_MS) {
            lastCrashTime = now;
            triggerCrashAlarm();
            sendTelemetry("crash");  // Immediate crash event
        }
        else if (currentG > HARD_BRAKE_G_THRESHOLD &&
                 (now - lastCrashTime) > CRASH_COOLDOWN_MS) {
            lastCrashTime = now;
            sendTelemetry("hard_brake");
        }
    }

    // ── Send normal telemetry at regular interval ────────
    if (now - lastTelemetry >= TELEMETRY_INTERVAL_MS) {
        lastTelemetry = now;
        sendTelemetry("normal");
    }

    // ── Turn off buzzer after duration ────────────────────
    if (buzzerOffTime > 0 && now > buzzerOffTime) {
        digitalWrite(PIN_BUZZER, LOW);
        digitalWrite(PIN_LED, LOW);
        buzzerOffTime = 0;
    }
}
📤
To flash: Open firmware.ino in Arduino IDE → Select board "ESP32 Dev Module" → Select COM port → Click Upload (→ arrow button). Hold the BOOT button on ESP32 if upload fails.

12 Troubleshooting

Common issues and how to fix them.

Problem Cause Fix
ESP32 LED blinks rapidly on boot MPU6050 not detected Check I2C wiring: SDA→GPIO21, SCL→GPIO22. Verify 3.3V power. Try swapping SDA/SCL.
Wi-Fi won't connect Wrong SSID/password or 5GHz network ESP32 only supports 2.4GHz Wi-Fi. Double-check SSID and password in config.h. Use phone hotspot as fallback.
WebSocket shows "Disconnected" loop M2 server not running or wrong IP Start M2 first: python -m uvicorn main:app --port 8000. Verify SERVER_IP in config.h matches your laptop's IP (ipconfig).
GPS always shows default coordinates No satellite fix (indoor) GPS needs clear sky. Take the car outside. First fix takes 30–90 seconds (cold start). Check GPS module LED: blinking = searching, steady = fixed.
G-force reads 0.0 or NaN MPU6050 I2C communication failure Run I2C scanner sketch to verify device at address 0x68. Check wire connections. Try shorter jumper wires.
False crash detections Vibration from car motors Increase CRASH_G_THRESHOLD to 3.0 or higher. Mount MPU6050 on foam tape to dampen vibrations. Increase CRASH_COOLDOWN_MS.
Upload fails — "Failed to connect" ESP32 not in flash mode Hold BOOT button on ESP32, click Upload, release BOOT when "Connecting…" appears. Also try a different USB cable (must be data cable, not charge-only).
M4 map doesn't show crash pin M3 WebSocket not connected to M4 Verify M3 is running on port 4000. Check browser console on M4 for WS errors.
Buzzer doesn't sound Passive buzzer (needs PWM) or wiring Use an active buzzer (has built-in oscillator). Check polarity: + to GPIO4, – to GND.
ESP32 keeps restarting Power supply too weak Use a power bank that outputs at least 1A. Don't power ESP32 from laptop USB during wireless operation — use dedicated power bank.

void setup() {

        }
